# 1. Purpose

This programme develops a high-level, agent-in-the-world formulation of Trident-G and turns it into a sequence of falsifiable computational models and papers.

The central problem is:

> **How should a finite, embodied and developmentally changing agent regulate present satisfaction, defended viability, information seeking, commitment, capability construction and future controllable optionality when both its values and environmental affordances change?**

The programme combines five levels that should remain distinct:

```text
EXTENDED EVOLUTIONARY PROCESSES
selection, developmental bias, plasticity, niche construction,
multiple inheritance and reciprocal causation

                    ↓↑

ALLOSTATIC REGULATION
anticipated demand, defended variables, reserve,
resource allocation and viability

                    ↓↑

TRIDENT-G META-CONTROL
open, constrain, commit, compare, reopen and bank

                    ↓↑

LOCAL DECISION AND LEARNING COMPUTATIONS
valuation, reinforcement learning, predictive mapping,
evidence accumulation and epistemic policy evaluation

                    ↓↑

NICHE ACTION, CONSTRUCTION AND TRANSMISSION
action changes the agent, its affordance graph,
its banked capabilities and the structures available to later agents
```

The intended contribution is not that Trident-G replaces expected utility, diffusion models, reinforcement learning or active inference. The stronger and more defensible proposition is:

> **Trident-G provides a state-dependent meta-control architecture within which familiar decision, learning, evidence-accumulation and policy-evaluation formalisms can occupy local functional roles, serve as alternative implementations, or emerge as limiting cases. Extended evolutionary theory explains how the agent, its plasticity, inherited resources and constructed niche arise and change across developmental, cultural and evolutionary time.**

# 2. Scientific positioning and claims boundary

## 2.1 Programme type

The initial work is a **normative computational theory and optimality-analysis programme** using synthetic environments and known-truth simulations.

It may establish that:

- the proposed architecture is computationally coherent;
- different fixed decision strategies have identifiable ecological boundary conditions;
- state-dependent meta-control can dominate fixed strategies across a sufficiently heterogeneous distribution of environments, after its costs are included;
- viable optionality has conditional rather than universal value;
- capability banking can reduce later search costs while preserving reopening after change;
- niche construction and inherited external structures can alter which strategies are adaptive;
- explicit EES-inspired mechanisms generate distinguishable simulation predictions.

It may **not** establish from simulation alone that:

- human brains implement the proposed controller;
- simulated entropy is a direct measure of neural criticality;
- FPCN-A/B implements the simulated arbitration;
- a literal Trident cusp exists in neural or behavioural data;
- the model explains psychometric `g`;
- human evolution selected the exact simulated objective;
- epigenetic inheritance transmits acquired cognitive policies in humans;
- Trident-G interventions increase general intelligence or far transfer.

A successful scientific result may favour a simpler architecture or reject a proposed Trident mechanism.

## 2.2 Relation to the repository’s existing programme

The current repository programme asks whether candidate behavioural control architectures are identifiable, predictive and transportable to wrapper-transfer outcomes.

The AO programme asks a different, complementary question:

> **Under what ecological, allostatic, developmental and inheritance conditions should an adaptive agent deploy different control modes in the first place?**

The two programmes may later meet at the level of prospective behavioural predictions, but AO simulations must not redefine, retune or contaminate the current M-series studies.

Use:

```text
M-series
empirical and synthetic validation of candidate behavioural architectures

AO-series
normative agentic simulations and evolutionary-developmental optimality analyses
```

# 3. Core theoretical proposal

## 3.1 Agentic definition of general adaptive intelligence

> **General adaptive intelligence is the capacity of an embodied agent to regulate present satisfaction, defended viability, epistemic uncertainty, policy commitment and future controllable optionality while learning and modifying its position in a changing niche.**

Trident-G proposes that the agent does this by:

```text
opening candidate state and policy space
→ constraining candidates by evidence, relevance and viability
→ committing sufficiently to act
→ obtaining environmental feedback
→ retaining, revising or reopening the active model
→ banking portable capability
→ re-entering later search from a richer position
```

A decision is therefore not only selection from a fixed menu. It is an **allostatically gated regulation of closure over a dynamically constructed future-state space**.

## 3.2 Adaptive closure

A local decision is a temporary closure of a distribution over candidate models or policies.

Good closure is:

- sufficiently constrained to produce action;
- proportionate to evidence and stakes;
- sensitive to delay and opportunity cost;
- reversible where uncertainty remains consequential;
- capable of reopening after diagnostic mismatch;
- informative enough to improve later decisions.

This programme uses **adaptive closure** as the bridge between local decision kernels and the wider Trident meta-controller.

## 3.3 Generative optionality

Generative optionality is not passive diversification or raw branch count.

It is:

> **The maintained capacity to create, preserve and selectively control viable future paths by acquiring capabilities, resources, information, relationships or environmental affordances.**

A possibility becomes an actionable option only when the agent has enough capability, access, resource and niche support to exercise it.

Distinguish:

```text
POSSIBILITY
represented or imagined reachability

OPTION
actionable reachability

EMPOWERMENT
controllable reachability

GENERATIVE OPTIONALITY
capacity to create or strengthen future actionable and controllable paths
```

## 3.4 The central trade-off

The model must regulate at least four action values:

```text
ACCESS
enter a satisfying or valued state now

MAINTAIN
preserve current viability, valued basins and return routes

GENERATE
build capabilities or affordances that expand later attainable value

LEARN
gain information about the environment, transition structure or the agent’s values
```

Commitment remains essential. An agent that continually constructs options but never enters valued states or closes on a policy is not adaptively intelligent.

# 4. Explanatory levels

| Level | Primary question | Main processes | Trident-G interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evolutionary ecology** | Why does this class of architecture persist across ecological distributions? | Selection, drift, mutation/recombination, bet-hedging, multilevel conflict | Outer tuning of broad control architecture and reaction norms |
| **Developmental construction** | How is the individual phenotype built and calibrated? | Plasticity, developmental bias, learning, epigenetic/cellular regulation, socialisation | Development of Ψ-band accessibility, meta-efficacy, coherence, niche coupling, capacity and Gc geometry |
| **Allostasis** | What must this organism anticipate, protect and resource now? | Predictive regulation, defended variables, reserve, urgency, anticipated demand | State-dependent viability envelope and exploration/commitment budget |
| **Trident meta-control** | When should cognition open, constrain, commit, reopen or bank? | Entropy–constraint regulation, adaptive closure, prediction-error response | G-loop and Gf → Gc → Gf spiral |
| **Local computations** | How are value, evidence, transitions and actions computed now? | Utility evaluation, RL, SR planning, diffusion, epistemic value | Specialised kernels, implementations or limiting cases |
| **Niche action** | How does action alter the agent’s future state-space? | Affordance use, tool use, skill learning, relationship formation, environmental modification | Niche coupling and action-dependent successor graph |
| **Inheritance/transmission** | What persists beyond one episode, individual or generation? | Genetic, epigenetic/cellular, behavioural, cultural, ecological and digital transmission | Internal, externalised and institutional Gc with channel-specific fidelity and reconstruction |

These levels must not be collapsed into one optimiser. Evolutionary success is an ultimate explanatory criterion, allostasis is a within-lifetime regulatory process, and endorsed human values remain proximate objectives rather than disguised reproductive fitness.

# 5. Local computational formalisms

| Formalism | Possible local role inside the architecture | What Trident-G adds |
|---|---|---|
| **Expected utility** | Evaluate candidate outcomes or policies under current beliefs and preferences | Explains how the option set, viability constraints, value weights, capabilities and future affordances change—and when wider search should reopen |
| **Diffusion/evidence accumulation** | Convert sequential evidence into a thresholded local commitment | Regulates the threshold, candidate set and reopening policy according to allostatic state, error cost, urgency, reversibility and enactability |
| **Model-free reinforcement learning** | Learn efficient cached action values, routines or habits | Interpretable as fast Gc; Trident-G specifies when cached control is appropriate and when mismatch should reactivate Gf search |
| **Model-based RL / successor representation** | Learn transitions, reachability, expected occupancy and policy consequences | Supplies the predictive map over which Gf search, option evaluation and future-state reasoning operate |
| **Successor features** | Separate predicted future feature occupancy from changing reward/value weights | Supports rapid revaluation when the niche remains stable but the agent’s needs or values change |
| **Active inference** | Evaluate policies using pragmatic and epistemic consequences, or serve as a competing whole-agent implementation | Trident-G adds its proposed entropy–constraint dynamics, allostatic operating regimes, transfer validation, adaptive closure and Gf → Gc → Gf banking |
| **Empowerment / attainable utility** | Measure future controllability or preservation of achievable objectives | Constrains these by viability, present and possible future values, capability cost, route independence and selective commitment |
| **Resource-rational meta-control** | Choose computations in relation to their expected value and cost | Treats cognitive and physiological resources as changing stocks that action may consume, protect or expand |

Active inference should not be described as merely a subordinate local model. It should be treated as a benchmark whole-agent competitor, a possible implementation of selected computations, or a neighbouring formalism whose decomposition is compared with the Trident decomposition.

# 6. Extended evolutionary synthesis extension

The EES component is an explanatory extension, not a separate local decision rule.

## 6.1 Developmental plasticity and developmental bias

The agent is not born with a fully fixed decision architecture. Development and experience shape:

- the accessible range of control policies;
- exploration and commitment reaction norms;
- allostatic calibration;
- value learning and preference development;
- successor-map depth;
- capability acquisition;
- which variants are likely to be generated in later search.

Computationally, developmental plasticity should be represented as structured parameter change rather than unrestricted learning:

```math
\Theta_{i,t+1}
=
\Theta_{i,t}
+
\mathcal P(\Theta_{i,t}, X_{i,t}, a_{i,t}, o_{i,t}, D_i)
```

where `D_i` specifies a developmental reaction norm or plasticity architecture.

Developmental bias is expressed when some policy variations, state representations or capability combinations are easier to generate than others.

## 6.2 Niche construction and ecological inheritance

The environment is an action-dependent affordance graph rather than a fixed transition matrix.

```math
\mathcal G_{t+1}
=
F_{\mathcal G}
(\mathcal G_t, a_t, c_{t+1}, A_t^{collective}, \zeta_t)
```

Actions can:

- create or remove edges;
- alter transition probabilities;
- create shared resources;
- change gatekeeper dependence;
- modify cue and feedback structures;
- leave tools, institutions or infrastructure to later agents.

Ecological inheritance occurs when these modifications persist beyond the action or actor that created them.

## 6.3 Inclusive and multiple inheritance

Use the more precise phrase **multiple inheritance, transmission and reconstruction systems**, not “different kinds of replication” as a blanket term.

| Channel | Candidate model object | Main parameters | Trident-G correlate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Genetic** | Broad architecture and reaction-norm parameters | mutation, recombination, heritability, selection | inherited control architecture and plasticity bounds |
| **Epigenetic/cellular** | Slow regulatory or developmental calibration variables | induction, persistence, reset, intergenerational transmission probability | candidate calibration of allostatic or plasticity parameters |
| **Behavioural** | Demonstrated routines and action policies | observability, imitation, practice, fidelity | socially acquired fast Gc |
| **Cultural/symbolic** | Language, theories, norms, methods and role systems | teaching, prestige/conformity bias, reconstruction, innovation | shared mindware and slow schematic Gc |
| **Ecological** | Tools, paths, institutions and modified affordance graphs | persistence, access, maintenance, exclusion | inherited niche structure |
| **Digital/technological** | Code, databases, prompts, models and external memory | copying fidelity, versioning, access, platform dependence | externalised H-AGI/technological Gc |

Each channel requires its own fidelity, transformation, persistence and access model. Social learning and cultural transmission are often reconstructive rather than exact copying.

## 6.4 Epigenetic boundary

Epigenetic regulation may be modelled as a candidate mechanism of slow developmental and allostatic calibration.

The first simulations should **not** assume that acquired cognitive policies are reliably transmitted transgenerationally through human epigenetic inheritance.

A defensible initial implementation is:

```text
within-lifetime regulatory persistence
+
optional intergenerational transmission probability
+
explicit sensitivity analysis from zero upward
```

This allows the model to ask when such persistence would matter without presupposing that it is common or established in humans.

## 6.5 Gene–culture coevolution and externalised Gc

Cultural and technological structures can change the developmental niche and therefore the selective value of cognitive policies.

Examples include:

- teaching practices;
- writing and external memory;
- scientific methods;
- institutions and professional roles;
- AI-supported reasoning protocols;
- digital platforms and access rules.

The computational question is not merely whether a policy is copied. It is whether externalised Gc:

- reduces individual search cost;
- expands or narrows future policy space;
- improves transfer;
- creates dependence on one infrastructure;
- changes the value of underlying unaided capability;
- accelerates adaptation or produces cultural lock-in.

## 6.6 Bet-hedging and conditional phenotype switching

Fluctuating environments may favour:

- persistent specialisation;
- stochastic diversification;
- within-lifetime conditional switching;
- mixed strategies across a population;
- generative capability that allows new policies to be built after change.

Trident-G predicts that a conditional meta-controller should be favoured when cues are informative enough for state-dependent switching, while population-level bet-hedging may remain useful when environmental changes are difficult to predict.

## 6.7 Evolvability and modularity

The Gf → Gc → Gf spiral can be tested as a cognitive form of evolvability.

Good Gc modules should:

- reduce repeated search cost;
- preserve boundary conditions;
- recombine under new Gf demand;
- survive some wrapper changes;
- remain reopenable after mismatch.

Bad Gc should produce rigid local optimisation and low adaptability.

## 6.8 Multilevel selection and conflict

The model may later include selection or optimisation pressures at several levels:

```text
gene or inherited element
individual organism
family or coalition
organisation or institution
cultural lineage
```

Objectives can conflict. A structure that increases organisational persistence may reduce individual agency or viability. A tool that improves immediate group performance may erode individual learning.

Do not label an organisation an evolutionary individual unless reproduction, inheritance and differential persistence are explicitly represented.

## 6.9 Replicator, interactor and reproducer distinctions

The programme should distinguish:

- what stores or transmits structure;
- what embodied system interacts with the environment;
- what entity, if any, reproduces as a unit;
- what is reconstructed anew during development or learning.

Not every persistent Gc structure is a literal replicator.

# 7. Formal agent and environment

## 7.1 Agent state

For individual `i`, generation `g` and within-lifetime time `t`:

```math
X_{g,i,t}
=
(s_{g,i,t},
 h_{g,i,t},
 r_{g,i,t},
 c_{g,i,t},
 w_{g,i,t},
 b_{g,i,t},
 \mathcal M_{g,i,t},
 d_{g,i,t},
 \Theta_{g,i,t})
```

where:

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `s` | Current external/situational state |
| `h` | Defended allostatic and viability variables |
| `r` | Available resources and slack |
| `c` | Skills, relationships, tools and other capabilities |
| `w` | Current needs, desires and value weights |
| `b` | Beliefs about transitions, values and environmental regimes |
| `M` | Banked internal Gc modules and policy repertoire |
| `d` | Developmental and slowly changing regulatory state |
| `Θ` | Meta-control and learning parameters |

The niche is represented separately as:

```math
\mathcal G_{g,t}
=
(\mathcal S, \mathcal E_{g,t}, P_{g,t}, Z_{g,t})
```

where `Z` contains persistent environmental, social, institutional or technological structures.

## 7.2 Satisfaction and evolving values

Each state has satisfaction-relevant features:

```math
\phi(s)
=
[pleasure, security, autonomy, relatedness, mastery, meaning, health, novelty, ...]
```

The agent has changing weights:

```math
u_t(s)
=
w_t^\top \phi(s)
```

A fuller model may include need, deprivation, satiation and adaptation:

```math
u_t(s)
=
u(\phi(s), w_t, d_t, h_t)
```

Distinguish predicted and experienced satisfaction:

```math
\delta_t^S
=
S_t^{experienced}
-
\widehat S_t
```

Current satisfaction is evidence about value, not the complete adaptive objective.

## 7.3 Viability envelope

Let `V_t` be the defended viable region:

```math
\mathcal V_t
=
\{h: h_j^{min}(t) \le h_j \le h_j^{max}(t)\}
```

A policy may be subject to a chance constraint:

```math
P(h_{t:t+H} \in \mathcal V_t)
\ge
1-\epsilon
```

Alternative implementations should compare:

- hard viability constraints;
- ruin penalties;
- lower-tail/CVaR protection;
- soft allostatic costs.

The conclusion must not be made dependent on one arbitrary viability formulation.

## 7.4 Action classes

The minimal action grammar is:

```text
ACCESS
enter a currently valued state

MAINTAIN
protect a resource, valued basin, relationship or route

PROBE
gain information about a transition, regime or preference

BUILD
acquire a capability or construct a future affordance

COMMIT
concentrate resources on a selected path, potentially closing alternatives

EXIT
leave an obsolete or deteriorating path

BANK
convert successful structure into reusable internal or external Gc

TRANSMIT
make a policy, structure or niche modification available to another agent
```

`TRANSMIT` is not required for the first within-lifetime paper.

# 8. Candidate within-lifetime objective

A provisional action/controller score is:

```math
\begin{aligned}
Q_t(a,m)=
&\ S_t(a)
-C_t^{allo}(a)
+\alpha_t IG_t(a)\\
&+\beta_t O_{H,t}^{V}(a)
+\kappa_t \Delta C_{H,t}(a)\\
&-\rho_t R_{H,t}^{ruin}(a)
-\omega_t C^{compute}_t(m)
-\mu_t C^{maintain}_t(a)
\end{aligned}
```

where:

| Term | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| `S` | Present or anticipated satisfaction |
| `C_allo` | Energetic, resource and regulatory cost |
| `IG` | Information gained about the niche or values |
| `O_H^V` | Viable, satisfaction-relevant and controllable future optionality |
| `ΔC_H` | Capability or reusable-policy growth |
| `R_ruin` | Catastrophic or unrecoverable downside |
| `C_compute` | Cost of invoking controller `m` |
| `C_maintain` | Cost of keeping the relevant option alive |

The defining claim is not the exact additive form. It is that the controller’s weights are state- and niche-dependent:

```math
(\alpha_t,\beta_t,\kappa_t,\rho_t,\omega_t,\mu_t)
=
f_\Theta(
allostatic\ reserve,
volatility,
uncertainty,
irreversibility,
time\ pressure,
niche\ coupling,
developmental\ state)
```

## 8.1 Optionality should initially remain multidimensional

Do not hide the result inside one hand-weighted optionality score.

Report at least:

- viable reachable-state coverage;
- satisfaction-relevant attainable value;
- empowerment/controllability;
- route independence and common-mode fragility;
- reversibility;
- capability growth;
- option-maintenance burden;
- information value;
- returnability to valued basins.

A scalar can be added only with explicit sensitivity analysis over its weights.

## 8.2 Flexibility premium

A candidate measure of retained flexibility across uncertain future niches and values is:

```math
OP_H(X_t)
=
\mathbb E_{\Theta_H}
\left[
\max_{\pi \in \Pi_t} V_H^\pi(X_t;\Theta_H)
\right]
-
\max_{\pi \in \Pi_t}
\mathbb E_{\Theta_H}
\left[
V_H^\pi(X_t;\Theta_H)
\right]
```

where `Θ_H` contains a future environmental regime and future value profile.

This is the value of retaining multiple policies until uncertainty about the future is resolved.

The generative optionality of action `a` is then:

```math
GO_H(a)
=
\mathbb E[OP_H(X_{t+1})\mid a]
-OP_H(X_t)
-C_{maintain}(a)
```

This measure should compete with simpler reachability, empowerment and attainable-utility measures.

# 9. Trident meta-controller

## 9.1 Mixture-of-controllers formulation

Let local controller `m` return action values `Q_m(a)`.

```math
Q_t(a)
=
\sum_m g_{m,t}Q_m(a)
```

with:

```math
g_t
=
softmax\left[
f_\Theta(
h_t,
r_t,
\widehat{volatility}_t,
\widehat{uncertainty}_t,
irreversibility_t,
horizon_t,
d_t)
\right]
```

Candidate local controllers include:

- cached/model-free;
- expected-value planning;
- model-based/SR planning;
- information-seeking;
- homeostatic preservation;
- option-preserving/empowerment;
- active-inference-like policy evaluation;
- diffusion-style commitment threshold.

Diffusion is a commitment kernel rather than a whole-agent objective.

## 9.2 Qualitative control rules

```text
low viability margin
→ protect the base and favour reliable Gc

high consequential uncertainty + adequate reserve
→ probe and widen model-based search

large diagnostic prediction error
→ reopen Gf search

stable niche + reliable policy
→ use compiled Gc or cached control

high future-value uncertainty
→ preserve reversible, cross-value capabilities

closing opportunity window
→ raise delay cost and commit

successful transferable action
→ bank capability and reduce later search cost

option maintenance cost exceeds future flexibility premium
→ prune the option
```

## 9.3 Entropy as a regulated variable

The theory should not claim unconditional entropy maximisation.

Compare:

```text
low future-state/path entropy
→ brittleness and lock-in

intermediate viable, relevant and controllable diversity
→ adaptive flexibility

excessive maintained diversity
→ weak convergence, high cost and delayed commitment
```

The expected relationship is conditional and often inverted-U shaped.

# 10. Evolutionary outer loop

## 10.1 Outer objective

The outer evolutionary process tunes broad meta-control and plasticity parameters across an ecological distribution:

```math
\Theta^*
=
arg\max_\Theta
\mathbb E_{\mathcal E}
[\log W(\Theta\mid\mathcal E)]
```

`W` may include survival, reproduction or lineage persistence in the simulation. The geometric/log form is a candidate way to penalise repeated catastrophic failure.

This is an explanatory model of architectural selection, not a claim that people consciously maximise fitness.

## 10.2 Evolved objects

Candidate evolved objects include:

- baseline exploration/constraint gain;
- sensitivity to allostatic reserve;
- commitment reaction norms;
- value-of-information sensitivity;
- ruin aversion;
- learning rates and volatility inference;
- capability-building propensity;
- option-maintenance tolerance;
- developmental plasticity parameters;
- social learning and transmission biases;
- propensity to construct or preserve niches.

## 10.3 Selection architectures to compare

- individual lifetime return;
- individual geometric-mean viability;
- lineage persistence;
- group productivity;
- mixed individual/group selection;
- cultural-object persistence.

This comparison is necessary because different outer criteria may select very different forms of “intelligence”.

# 11. Competing agents

## 11.1 Minimum first-paper agents

1. **Myopic satisfaction** — maximises immediate experienced value.
2. **Fixed expected-value planner** — maximises discounted value under fixed current preferences.
3. **Homeostatic preservation** — prioritises defended variables and returnability.
4. **Pure causal-entropy or empowerment agent** — maximises path diversity or controllability.
5. **Trident allostatic meta-controller** — changes access, preservation, probing, building and commitment weights according to state and niche.

## 11.2 Extended benchmark agents

Add only after the minimal tournament works:

- model-free RL;
- model-based/SR RL;
- attainable-utility preservation;
- successor-feature generalisation;
- resource-rational computation selection;
- fixed active-inference-like agent;
- oracle regime-selector;
- random and simple heuristic baselines.

## 11.3 Fairness rule

Each simpler agent must have environments in which it can win.

A credible result is a strategy phase diagram, not universal Trident superiority.

# 12. Limiting-case programme

A central paper claim should be demonstrated rather than stated rhetorically.

Show that restricted parameter settings or components recover familiar special cases:

```text
fixed values
+ fixed affordance graph
+ no information/optionality term
→ expected-value planning

cached values
+ temporal-difference updating
→ model-free RL

explicit transition model or SR
+ fixed reward
→ model-based predictive planning

sequential noisy evidence
+ fixed boundary
→ diffusion-like local commitment

epistemic value
+ preference-related policy value
→ active-inference-like evaluation

future controllability only
→ empowerment-like agent

auxiliary-objective attainability only
→ option-preservation agent
```

The meta-control advantage is supported only if:

```text
no single local controller dominates all registered regimes
```

and state-dependent arbitration approaches the regime-specific oracle after computational and maintenance costs are deducted.

# 15. Core falsifiable predictions

## P1. Stable-world specialisation

In stable environments with stable preferences and expensive option maintenance, focused expected-value or cached policies should outperform the Trident controller on immediate and possibly cumulative value.

## P2. Volatility crossover

As environmental volatility, preference drift or irreversibility increases, the Trident controller should show lower ruin, lower dynamic regret and faster recovery than fixed strategies.

## P3. Inverted-U optionality

Too little optionality should create brittleness; too much should create maintenance cost, weak selection and delayed commitment. The optimal point should shift with reserve and volatility.

## P4. Allostatic gating

The same objective environment should induce different optimal exploration and commitment policies as the agent’s viability margin changes.

## P5. Capability banking

Banked, bounded and reopenable Gc should reduce later search cost without eliminating adaptation after graph or value change.

## P6. Niche-construction advantage

Actions that create persistent, reusable affordances should show delayed and potentially convex returns, but only when construction and maintenance costs are below their later value.

## P7. False optionality

Agents with rich internal maps but poor niche calibration should overestimate accessible futures and perform poorly on realised enactment.

## P8. Route independence

A smaller number of independent paths should outperform a larger number of paths sharing one gatekeeper under common-mode shocks.

## P9. Evolved conditional control

Heterogeneous but partly predictable environments should select conditional allostatic entropy regulation rather than permanent exploitation, preservation or exploration.

## P10. Bet-hedging boundary

Unpredictable fluctuations should increase the advantage of population-level diversification or stochastic switching relative to cue-dependent within-lifetime meta-control.

## P11. Externalised-Gc trade-off

High-fidelity cultural or technological transmission should accelerate adaptation, but can produce lock-in or loss of unaided capability when external structures become over-dominant or obsolete.

## P12. Developmental calibration

Plastic reaction norms should outperform fixed architectures across changing developmental niches, but excessive plasticity should create instability or capture by transient environments.

| Ablation | Removed mechanism | Expected failure if the mechanism matters |
|---|---|---|
| No viability constraint | Defended base | High reward with elevated ruin |
| No entropy/opening | Alternative generation | Lock-in after change |

| No successor map | Multi-step reachability | Poor path evaluation |

| No capability banking | Gf → Gc → Gf | Repeated search from scratch |
| No reopening | Mismatch response | Policy hysteresis |
| No maintenance cost | Option pruning | Accumulation of useless alternatives |
| No future-value uncertainty | Future-self robustness | High regret after preference drift |

# 22. Open modelling decisions for AO0

These decisions should be settled before implementation:

1. **Satisfaction scale:** cardinal state utility, latent utility inferred from choice, or feature-based utility only?
2. **Viability:** hard constraint, ruin penalty, CVaR, or registered comparison among all three?
3. **Value change:** exogenous drift, homeostatic need change, experience-dependent preference learning, or separate variants?
4. **Reflective endorsement:** included in the first paper or reserved for a later normative extension?
5. **Optionality:** flexibility premium, empowerment, attainable utility, graph robustness, or multidimensional profile as the primary outcome?
6. **Entropy:** path entropy, policy entropy, belief entropy or generated candidate diversity? These must not be conflated.
7. **Mutual-information constraint:** objective term, candidate-filtering process, or controller-selection signal?
8. **Meta-controller:** hand-specified rule, learned gate, evolved reaction norm, or staged comparison?
9. **Active-inference comparator:** full implementation, simplified expected-free-energy agent, or conceptual comparison only in Paper 1?
10. **Evolutionary fitness:** survival, reproduction, geometric-mean return, lineage persistence, or multiple registered outer objectives?
11. **Niche construction:** how will graph modification be distinguished from delayed reward and capability growth?
12. **Transmission:** which structures count as copied, reconstructed, inherited environmentally or merely observed?
13. **Unaided capability:** how will externalised/digital Gc improve or erode internal capability?
14. **Collective level:** defer until single-agent identifiability is secure, or include one minimal public-goods niche?

# 23. Reproducibility and falsification rules

1. Register world families before confirmatory results are inspected.
2. Use separate development and held-out ecological distributions.
3. Preserve identical stochastic realisations across competing agents where possible.
4. Include computation and option-maintenance costs.
5. Do not tune only the Trident agent; give comparable tuning budgets to competitors.
6. Report environments where simpler agents win.
7. Report degenerate, unstable and failed fits.
8. Conduct sensitivity analysis over utility, viability and optionality definitions.
9. Do not infer biological truth from optimisation success.
10. Do not describe simulated inheritance channels as established human mechanisms.
11. Prefer simpler controllers when they match the richer architecture on held-out value, calibration, robustness and recovery.
12. Freeze code, configs, seeds, manifests and primary contrasts before confirmatory runs.
